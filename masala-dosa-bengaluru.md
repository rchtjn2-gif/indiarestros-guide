# Masala dosa in Bengaluru: prices, neighbourhoods and how to find a good one

Bengaluru's masala dosa is a breakfast institution: a crisp, golden crepe of fermented rice and urad dal, folded around spiced potato, served with sambar and chutneys. You can eat one standing at a busy darshini for the price of a bus ticket, or at a sit-down restaurant for several times that.

## What it costs

According to IndiaRestros, which reads restaurant menus, **48+ places in Bengaluru** list masala dosa on a menu on file, priced from **₹30 to ₹275**, with a **typical price of about ₹90**. Many of the cheapest options are quick-service "quick bites" places; the higher prices tend to be at full-service and multi-cuisine restaurants.

Menus name the dish in different ways: Masala Dosa, Masala Dosai, Masala Dose, Special Masala Dosa, Mini Masala Dosa or a Masala Dosa Combo. If you search, try a couple of spellings.

## Where it's most listed

On IndiaRestros the dish is most often listed in Nehru Nagar, Subramanyapura, HSR Layout and Jakkur, but menus with masala dosa turn up across the city, from Basavanagudi and Jayamahal to R.T. Nagar and Yeshwanthpura. These counts reflect menus on file, not a ranking of the best.

## Styles you'll meet

- **Darshini style:** fast, self-service, eaten standing. The dosa is often thicker and softer, with a generous smear of red chutney inside in some places.
- **Udupi style:** a thinner, crisper dosa with a lighter potato palya, served with coconut chutney and sambar.
- **Benne (butter) dosa:** associated with Davanagere, softer and rich with butter. Some Bengaluru menus list it separately from masala dosa.
- **Mini or combo plates:** smaller dosas paired with idli or vada for a mixed breakfast.

## How to spot a good one

1. **Go early.** Many tiffin places are busiest from 7 to 10 am, when batter and chutneys are freshest.
2. **Watch the tawa.** A steady stream of dosas means quick turnover.
3. **Taste the chutney.** Fresh coconut chutney tastes clean and bright; it goes flat when kept too long.
4. **Check the sambar.** It should be hot and freshly tempered.
5. **Look for the veg mark.** Masala dosa is a vegetarian dish; IndiaRestros shows veg marks taken from each menu.

## What to order with it

A filter coffee is the classic pairing. If you are hungry, add a plate of idli or a medu vada; many places sell them as a combo. Ask for extra chutney rather than sambar if you prefer your dosa crisp, since sambar poured over the top softens it quickly.

## Checking before you go

Timings change, especially on holidays. Call ahead for a weekend breakfast trip, and check whether a place is dine-in, takeaway or both.
