# A highway dhaba guide: how to pick a good stop

On a long Indian road trip, the dhaba stop is half the point. A good one gives you hot rotis, dal that has been simmering since morning and a clean place to stretch. A bad one gives you a stomach ache for the next 200 km. Here's how to tell them apart.

## Signs of a busy, fresh kitchen

- **Trucks and taxis parked outside.** Drivers on the same route every week know which kitchens are reliable. A full lot at an odd hour is the best review there is.
- **A tandoor that's actually lit.** Rotis made in front of you are fresh by definition.
- **Short menu, fast turnover.** A board with eight dishes that sell out beats a laminated menu with eighty.
- **Clean water source.** Bottled water sealed in front of you, and a hand-wash basin that works.

## What to order

Stick to what the kitchen makes in bulk and serves hot: dal (dal makhani in Punjab and Haryana, dal tadka almost everywhere), a seasonal sabzi, tandoori roti or paratha, and a glass of lassi or chai. On the Mumbai to Pune or Bengaluru routes, look for regional thalis instead. Leave the "Chinese" and continental items for the city.

## Finding dhabas on your route

Plan two or three possible stops before you leave rather than gambling when everyone is already hungry. IndiaRestros has a list of [dhabas across India](https://indiarestros.com/kind/dhaba) you can scan by area, and its [dish catalog](https://indiarestros.com/dishes) helps if you have a craving for something specific. Check opening times, since some highway places close in the afternoon lull, and save a phone number to call ahead.

## Small habits that help

- Eat a little earlier than usual so you're not choosing in a hurry.
- Carry your own tissues and hand sanitiser.
- Pay attention to how the cook handles raw and cooked food.
- If in doubt, order fewer dishes and ask for them fresh.
